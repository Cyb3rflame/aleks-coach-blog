Most club players drop the racket on their two-handed backhand far too early.

Zverev does the opposite. He holds his hands up in the takeback a little longer, and only drops them when he is ready to swing. That one change gives you easier power and a lot more control.

Here is the side-by-side.

## The mistake: dropping the racket straight away

![Zverev holding his hands up while a club player has already dropped the racket](/blog/two-handed-backhand-racket-drop/club-player-drops-racket-early-vs-zverev.webp "Left: Zverev still holding. Right: club player already dropping.")

The club player drops the racket straight away. As soon as the takeback is done, the racket head starts falling.

But look closer. His hands don't actually drop. Only the tip of the racket drops. The hands stay in the same position.

So there is almost no gravity helping the drop. Nothing is building momentum into the racket before the swing starts.

## What Zverev does differently

![Zverev at the top of his backhand takeback with his hands held high](/blog/two-handed-backhand-racket-drop/zverev-backhand-hands-held-high.webp "Zverev holds his hands up in the takeback")

Zverev stays in this position a little bit longer. Only when he is ready does he let the tip of the racket drop, and then swing towards the ball.

Watch his hands. They go all the way up, then he drops them.

![Zverev dropping his hands before swinging, with lines showing the drop](/blog/two-handed-backhand-racket-drop/zverev-backhand-hands-drop-gravity.webp "Hands drop from up here to down here")

That drop is gravity doing the work. It starts the racket moving and gives you more acceleration into contact, without you having to muscle it.

## The second problem: the racket tip pointing back

![Club player with the racket tip pointing towards the back fence](/blog/two-handed-backhand-racket-drop/club-player-racket-tip-points-back.webp "Racket tip pointing all the way back")

On the club player, the tip of the racket points all the way back towards the back fence.

That happens because he opens up a little or gets the wrist involved too much.

Zverev doesn't do that. He keeps the strings facing the same way through the takeback. That makes it much easier to control the racket and keeps the wrist out of it.

## Why it happens

Acceleration has to come from somewhere.

The club player's hands aren't high enough in the takeback. If he kept the racket the way Zverev does, he wouldn't have enough space to build speed.

So he compensates. He uses the wrist and lets the racket tip swing all the way back. That makes the swing bigger than it needs to be, and bigger swings are harder to time and control.

Fix the hands, and the wrist has no reason to get involved.

## The fix: hold it, then drop it

Two things.

**One, get the hands up in the takeback.** Not just the racket head. The hands themselves need to be high enough that they have somewhere to drop from.

**Two, hold it there a little bit longer.** Don't start dropping the moment the takeback is finished. Wait. Then let the hands drop and swing.

## Drills

**Pause at the top.** Shadow swing your backhand. At the end of the takeback, freeze for one second with the hands high. Then drop and swing. 15 reps. You are training the hold.

**Drop feed with a pause.** Have someone drop feed you, or self feed. Get into the takeback early, hold, then drop and hit. 20 balls. Don't worry about pace. Just feel the drop doing the work.

**Strings check.** Film your takeback from behind. At the top, the racket tip should not be pointing at the back fence. If it is, the wrist is getting involved. Keep the strings facing the same way and go again.

## The bottom line

Power on the two-handed backhand doesn't come from swinging harder. It comes from letting gravity start the swing for you.

Get the hands up. Hold it a little longer. Then drop it.

## FAQ

**Why do I drop the racket too early on my two-handed backhand?**
Usually because the hands aren't high enough in the takeback, so there is nowhere to drop from. Players then rush the drop or use the wrist to create speed. Getting the hands higher and holding the position a moment longer fixes most of it.

**What is the racket drop on a two-handed backhand?**
It is the moment the hands and racket fall from the takeback position before swinging forward. A good drop uses gravity to start the racket moving, which gives you more acceleration into contact with less effort.

**Should my racket tip point at the back fence on my backhand takeback?**
No. If the tip points all the way back, the wrist is usually too involved. That makes the swing bigger than it needs to be and harder to control. Keeping the strings facing the same way through the takeback, like Zverev, keeps the wrist quiet.

**How do I get more power on my two-handed backhand?**
Let gravity help. Get your hands up in the takeback, hold them there a little longer, then drop them as you start the swing. The drop builds momentum into the racket so you don't have to force the pace with your arms or wrist.
